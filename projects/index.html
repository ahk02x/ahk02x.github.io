Crucix — local-model lab

running

A side project for learning where a small open model running on my own GPU genuinely helps and where it only looks like it does. A 7B model reads a news sweep and takes a position on dated market questions; a no-drift model prices the same questions, and both are scored on the same outcomes, on paper only. 99 days, 7,538 model calls, roughly 11 GPU-hours. No edge so far: all of the apparent gain traces to a single instrument the news feed could only ever read one way, so the gate stays shut and that finding stands. Five hypotheses killed before any of them cost anything; a lab that can refuse its own idea is the entire point.

certguard

built

I found a bug in Go's certificate parser: a 260 KB certificate costs about 24 MB to read, before the server decides whether to trust it. I reported it, then built a small Go library that turns that certificate away for about 100 bytes.

built

The same open AI models on a gaming GPU, an older GPU and my laptop. The laptop beat a real graphics card 11 to 1 on a model that didn't fit on the card. Before you ask how fast, ask whether it fits.

flowtrace

tech debt

Walks every conversation in a packet capture through the DNS, TCP, TLS and application stages and shows where it stalled. Silence and refusal are different failures, and where the silence starts names the guilty layer.

Inbox hub

tech debt

WhatsApp and Telegram land in one chat, each message sorted into a tier from urgent to mute, with a line saying why it reached me and buttons to re-tier the sender. Voice notes are transcribed locally. Parked one step short: pairing.

Helmet HUD

tech debt

An AI copilot for a motorcycle helmet. The helmet stays dumb, just a mic, speakers and a small display, and the AI lives on the phone. The first rung is voice only, no optics, and one rule is already set: never drill the shell. Parked until I have a bike again.

Brain-computer interface

tech debt

Look at one of four flickering tiles and it reads which one from your brain signal, then switches a light. The code is done and tested on synthetic signals, and the trigger was tightened until fourteen idle minutes gave zero false fires. Parked on hardware: it needs electrodes at the back of the head, which a consumer headband doesn't reach.
