SQ5 · how the data is captured · the box in the footwell

The car telemetry logger

A Raspberry Pi 4 in the passenger footwell runs the logger on every drive. This box is the whole data path into the car: 58 engine-computer channels, about two and a half times a second, off the car's own 500 kbps bus. The reverse-engineered tuning cable on the overview is how the channel map was worked out, and it is not part of this box. Here it is taken apart, from the four lid screws down to the printed base.

01 / Anatomy · seven layers

It comes apart in seven moves.

Fig. 1 · Isometric t 0.00 · yaw 45.0°
AssembledExploded
  1. 01

    Chassis screws #6-32 × 6

    Four steel pan-heads, the only fasteners and the only loose metal in the box. Each one passes through the lid and takes 4.0 mm of thread in a printed corner boss.

  2. 02

    Honeycomb lid

    Black PLA, 4.0 mm thick. 102 whole 6 mm hexes, 3180 mm² of open area, so the box runs without a fan. The red S and Q are pressed in flush, 2.4 mm deep, and the Q's counter stays black lid.

  3. 03

    DS3231 RTC

    Keeps time while the car is off. A small black board that presses straight onto the end of the GPIO header, with a yellow coin cell under a spring clip. It runs on 3.3 V, never 5 V: at 5 V its pull-ups would put 5 V on the Pi's I2C bus.

  4. 04

    Raspberry Pi 4 Rev 1.1

    Runs the capture and push services, held down by its own M2 × 4 screws, because the first case trusted printed legs and they snapped. 2.886 W at idle and 0.1145 W halted, a 96% cut.

  5. 05

    CANable2 adapter

    USB to CAN on slcan firmware, listening on the 500 kbps bus. Draws 0.235 W. Its green screw terminal points at the back wall.

  6. 06

    OBD-II pigtail

    Three wires off the adapter's green terminal, through a zip-tie strain relief and out the back wall to the diagnostic port: CAN-H, CAN-L and ground. The +12 V pin stays unconnected.

  7. 07

    Printed base

    99.8 × 88.8 × 30.8 mm, 106.8 × 95.8 across the corner ears. Honeycomb on every wall under a solid rim band, one side left open for the Pi's ports, and four tapped corner posts that take the lid screws.

The case is fanless by evidence, not by hope: 121,072 temperature samples over two weeks, a maximum of 67.7 °C on the chip, zero throttling. The first case failed on measurement, not material. I left 1.2 mm of clearance for a board edge that sticks out 1.2 mm. That is zero clearance, and the hold-down legs snapped. This one is written in OpenSCAD with fit assertions that caught four real collisions before anything was printed. Rule since then: when adding a port, model the connector, not just the board.

Photos of the real box, printed and installed, are in the gallery. What the car itself has been doing is on the overview.

02 / Pinout · OBD-II

Three wires in, one pin left open.

The pigtail takes CAN-H, CAN-L and ground from the diagnostic port. Pin 16 carries +12 V from the battery, and this pigtail leaves it unconnected on purpose: the adapter runs on USB power from the Pi.
