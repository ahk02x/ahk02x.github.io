About

Hi, I'm Amir.

Engineer. Relentless tinkerer. Toronto.

By day I keep production payment and identity platforms running: the networks, the certificates, the deployments and the 2 a.m. root cause. I'm good at it. But everything I fix there belongs to someone else.

So at home I build things that are mine, end to end. My own servers, my own network, my own data, my own tools. If I own it, I understand it, and if I understand it, nobody can take it from me.

Where I've worked

An honours degree in software engineering from York University's Lassonde School of Engineering, then seven years moving down the stack and up in responsibility.

Cloud & software
Started as a systems administrator on a large cloud platform: hundreds of customers on Linux and Windows, VMware automated with PowerShell and Python (it cut the team's manual work by about a third), and scripted hardening of 100+ servers to a healthcare compliance standard. Later, upgrades and migrations of an enterprise order-management platform. Alongside the day job I was president of the intern community: 600 interns across offices in two provinces, 40 social and career events, 20 site leads reporting in each week, and a budget we never went over.
Telecom
Kept mobile provisioning flowing for millions of LTE and 5G subscribers. Wrote the outage playbooks the team recovered with, held the platform portfolio at a 98% success rate, and built a tool that took repetitive cleanup off the frontline network operations team.
Financial institutions
The part I'm proudest of. I worked with every bank and credit union on the platform, owning the hub service that sat in the middle of them. For each partner I wrote the configuration, raised the change, and carried it through every gate: dev, UAT, production. I ran major release migrations, wrote the automation and scripts that made all of it repeatable, rotated certificates without dropping traffic, and worked the hard tickets: mTLS, SAML and OIDC federation, HSM signing, and site-to-site VPNs that drop for no reason until you find the reason. Nomad, Consul, Vault, Ansible, AWS and Bitbucket pipelines, across two data centres, in a regulated environment where a mistake is somebody's payment.

Most-used tools: Linux, Ansible, Nomad, Consul, Vault, Docker, Helm, Splunk, Elastic, TLS and PKI, Keycloak, F5, FortiGate, IPsec, AWS, Azure, Python, Bash, PowerShell. Certified in Kubernetes and Terraform on AWS, Azure Fundamentals and CyberArk PAM.

How I work

  • Take it apart first. Read the wire, open the device, prove it with a tiny script, then build around it.
  • Evidence over vibes. A check that ran is not a check that passed. Unknown never means healthy.
  • Plain words. If the alert or the report needs a glossary, it failed.
  • AI as a tool I hold to account. I build with Claude and GPT every day, inside guardrails I wrote: an alert board the agent has to answer, a memory it has to keep honest, a cage it can't leave. The grid on the home page is that work, counted.
