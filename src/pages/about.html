About

Hi, I'm Amir.

I keep production systems running for a living. Then I go home and take apart things that were working fine.

For seven years I've looked after the parts of a system nobody notices until they break: payments moving between banks, phones joining a mobile network, a login page that dies when a certificate expires. I like the work. None of it is mine, though, so at home I build things that are. I run my own servers and my own network, I write my own tools, and I keep finding new ways to measure my car.

How it started

In July 2025 I asked a chatbot if it had ever seen Iron Man. Next thing I knew, I was trying to build my own Jarvis in a Windows terminal. Version 0.1 answered almost everything with one line: “I heard you, but I'm still learning. Let's build me smarter.” I typed “today was a breakthrough” into it. It didn't understand that either.

I wanted it because the assistant I'd been talking to belonged to a company that could change it or switch it off whenever it liked. I wanted one that ran on my own machine, kept its own memory, and taught me things while I built it. I still want that.

A year later jarvis is a real computer in my office, put together from leftover parts, and it keeps the master copy of every drive my car logs. The next step is my own language model on hardware I own. It already has a name, ahkira (subject to change).

The day job

An honours degree in software engineering, then seven years across three industries.

Cloud & software
Started as a systems administrator on a large cloud platform, automating VMware with PowerShell and Python and hardening 100+ servers to a healthcare compliance standard. Later, upgrades and migrations of an enterprise order-management platform. On the side I was president of the intern community: 600 interns, 40 events, and a budget we never went over.
Telecom
Kept mobile provisioning running for millions of LTE and 5G subscribers. Wrote the outage playbooks and the patch procedures, and held the platform at a 98% success rate.
Financial institutions
The part I'm proudest of. I owned the hub service that sat between every bank and credit union on the platform. I onboarded each partner from dev to production, ran the major release migrations, rotated certificates without dropping traffic, and worked the hard tickets: mTLS, SAML and OIDC federation, HSM signing, and VPN tunnels that drop for no reason until you find the reason. I also ran the endpoint side: Intune, Autopilot and an organization-wide Windows 11 migration.

Most-used tools: Linux, Ansible, Nomad, Consul, Vault, Docker, Helm, Splunk, Elastic, TLS and PKI, Keycloak, F5, FortiGate, IPsec, AWS, Azure, CyberArk, Python, Bash, PowerShell. Self-taught Kubernetes and Terraform on AWS. Three years as a scrum master.
