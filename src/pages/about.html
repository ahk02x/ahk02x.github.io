About

Hi, I'm Amir.

Engineer. Relentless tinkerer.

By day I'm an application operations and infrastructure engineer. I keep production payment and identity platforms running: the networks, the certificates, the deployments and the 2 a.m. root cause. I onboard new partners, carry every change from dev through UAT to production, run the release migrations, rotate certificates without dropping traffic, and automate whatever I catch myself doing twice. I'm good at it. But everything I fix, migrate, enhance or create there belongs to someone else.

So at home I build things that are mine, end to end. My own servers, my own network, my own data, my own tools. If I own it, I understand it, and if I understand it, nobody can take it from me.

Where I've worked

An honours degree in software engineering, where my first team lead role was a Java app that teaches children Braille, then seven years moving down the stack and up in responsibility.

Cloud & software
Started as a systems administrator on a large cloud platform: hundreds of customers on Linux and Windows, VMware automated with PowerShell and Python (it cut the team's manual work by about a third), scripted hardening of 100+ servers to a healthcare compliance standard, and network changes on Cisco switches and ASA firewalls. Later, upgrades and migrations of an enterprise order-management platform. Alongside the day job I was president of the intern community: 600 interns across offices in two provinces, 40 social and career events, 20 site leads reporting in each week, and a budget we never went over.
Telecom
Kept mobile provisioning flowing for millions of LTE and 5G subscribers. Wrote the outage playbooks the team recovered with, held the platform portfolio at a 98% success rate, wrote the patch procedures for on-prem and cloud systems and walked them through change review, worked product defects through with outside vendors, and built a tool that took repetitive cleanup off the frontline network operations team.
Financial institutions
The part I'm proudest of. I worked with every bank and credit union on the platform, owning the hub service that sat in the middle of them. For each partner I wrote the configuration, raised the change, and carried it through every gate: dev, UAT, production. I ran major release migrations, wrote the automation and scripts that made all of it repeatable, rotated certificates without dropping traffic, and worked the hard tickets: mTLS, SAML and OIDC federation, HSM signing, and site-to-site VPNs that drop for no reason until you find the reason. Nomad, Consul, Vault, Ansible, AWS and Bitbucket pipelines, across two data centres, in a regulated environment where a mistake is somebody's payment. I also ran the endpoint side: Microsoft 365, Azure AD single sign-on, Intune and Autopilot, testing and rollout for an organization-wide Windows 11 migration, and vulnerability remediation held at 90% within SLA.

Most-used tools: Linux, Ansible, Nomad, Consul, Vault, Docker, Helm, Splunk, Elastic, TLS and PKI, Keycloak, F5, FortiGate, IPsec, AWS, Azure, Python, Bash, PowerShell. Certified in Kubernetes and Terraform on AWS, Azure Fundamentals and CyberArk PAM. Three years as a scrum master.

How I work

  • Take it apart first. Read the wire, open the device, prove it with a tiny script, then build around it.
  • Evidence over vibes. A check that ran is not a check that passed. Unknown never means healthy.
  • Plain words. If the alert or the report needs a glossary, it failed.
  • AI as a tool I hold to account. I build with Claude and GPT every day, inside guardrails I wrote: an alert board the agent has to answer, a memory it has to keep honest, a cage it can't leave. The grid on the home page is that work, counted.
