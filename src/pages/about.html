Amir Khademi / About me

Hi, I'm Amir.

I like knowing how things work. Especially the things I use every day.

That includes the car I drive, the servers in my office, and the tools I build when something keeps annoying me. A small question has a habit of turning into a project.

I'm a site reliability engineer. Away from work, you'll usually find me somewhere between a terminal, the car and a 3D printer.

Something
of my own.

At work, I help keep systems running that people depend on. At home, I want to build things I can keep, understand and change myself. That's why there's a lab in my office, why I save my project history, and why I keep coming back to local software.

One early attempt was a little assistant I called Jarvis. In a Windows terminal, I taught it to say hello. Then I tried to get it to remember things. It was rough, and I kept going back with the next error and the next idea.

That still feels like the right way to learn: pick something I actually want, get a small part working, and keep asking questions. The long-term ambition is a personal assistant running on hardware I control, with a memory I can take with me.

A few things
that explain me.

The car became a project.

I love my SQ5 as a car. But once I started looking at its data, I wanted to understand what was happening between the dashboard lights. That curiosity grew into SQ: drive logs, a Raspberry Pi, printed enclosures, and a lot more questions. It's something I can test against my own daily life.

Sometimes it's just a part that won't fit.

A washer-fluid adapter sent me through round after round of small changes: the lip, the neck, the filter tabs. The notches needed to stay square. It sounds tiny until you're holding the part and it still isn't right. I enjoy that connection between an idea on a screen and something I can actually use.

I want to see the evidence.

A certificate parser led me from an unexpected memory cost to a report to the Go team, a public issue, and a small library called certguard. I like following a question far enough to make it reproducible. The write-up includes the measurements and the limits of what they show.

The work
behind the hobbies.

My background is in cloud infrastructure, telecom and financial systems. The common thread is figuring out why something failed, getting it running again, and making the next recovery less painful.

Cloud infrastructure
Linux and Windows systems, virtualisation, automation and hardening. This is where I learned how much repeated manual work a useful script can remove.
Telecom
Mobile provisioning, outage playbooks, patching and working through defects with vendors. Keeping the service moving while finding the cause.
Financial systems
Partner integrations, release migrations, certificates and identity. Work that needs a clear change plan, a recovery path and someone to follow it through.

Linux, Python, Bash, PowerShell, Ansible, containers, observability and PKI are recurring tools in that work. More on LinkedIn →

Away from
the terminal.

Cars, the gym, soccer and pickleball. And 3D printing, although that tends to bring me straight back to the desk with another idea. I like making useful things, whether that's a part for the car or a better way to arrange the lab.

This site is where I keep the projects and the stories behind them. Some are useful, some are experiments, and some are still taking shape. If one of them overlaps with something you're working on, I'd enjoy hearing about it.
