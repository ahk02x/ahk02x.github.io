The hardware I run

Humble beginnings. Most of it is second-hand or rescued, and all of it is mine to break. The goal: my own datacenter, run from my room.

Network

  • blackwall: a Dell Vostro 460 (i5-2400, 8 GB) turned into an OPNsense firewall. It does DNS, DHCP and traffic accounting for the house, and it is the only thing standing between the house and the internet.
  • Switching: an Aruba Instant On 1930 managed switch, and a spare router working as a WiFi access point.
  • What it counts: traffic inside the house and traffic leaving it are kept as two different facts, because a house full of IoT devices should have to explain itself. How that works.

The firewall is a 2011 office PC

It came out of an office, not a shop, and opened up it is as plain as it looks: one Sandy Bridge i5, 8 GB, no graphics card, nothing exotic. The original Dell power supply died and took the whole house's internet with it, so a Corsair RM650x went in. That is the entire repair history of the machine that runs the network.

Rescuing hardware is the point rather than a constraint. Everything it does — DNS, DHCP, flow accounting — is software I can read, on a box nobody else wanted.

Servers

jarvis is the one that matters: Ryzen 5 5600GE, 32 GB DDR4, an RTX 2070 SUPER, a 1 TB NVMe for the system, and three 4 TB IronWolf drives in RAIDZ1 — about 6.9 TB usable, all on ZFS, so one drive can fail without taking the pool.

It runs Proxmox VE with Home Assistant and nine containers: a local language model, speech-to-text, Git hosting, a password manager, a file share and the car's data pipeline. The graphics card is there for the model and the transcription, not for games. About 111 W at idle, which is the number I actually watch, because it never turns off.

The box that runs the room

sentry is an Intel N5105 mini PC with 8 GB, and its job is the room rather than the network: the wall display, the Stream Deck and the lights. Keeping it off jarvis means the room can be rebooted without touching anything the house depends on.

It sits on a stand I printed, on top of blackwall. That stack is the most honest picture of this setup I have — a palm-sized mini PC balanced on a fifteen-year-old office tower, and between them they run the place.

Desk and wall

  • PHOSPHOR: a Dell U2719D hung in portrait. It shows the health of everything and latches alarms until I clear them. How it works.
  • Stream Deck MK.2: fifteen keys for the room, the lights and the PC's power.
  • AMIR-PC: Ryzen 9 3900X, RTX 3080 12 GB, 32 GB, Windows 11.
  • MacBook Pro 14: M3, 16 GB. Where everything gets written.

None of it arrived as a set. The photo is a build day: a BIOS flash running on a monitor on the floor, two machines open at once, and every cable in the room in play.

Car

  • Audi SQ5: a Raspberry Pi 4 in a 3D-printed case and a CANable 2.0 Pro on the diagnostic bus. It logs every drive to its own storage, and when the car is parked at home it joins the WiFi and sends the drives over to jarvis. Inside SQ.

Around the house

  • An LG C2 OLED, Google Home speakers and Govee lights, all driven through Home Assistant — my own code talking to the devices, with no vendor cloud in the path.

Where it's going

A datacenter in my room. NVIDIA DGX Sparks and RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell cards to build and run my own models, Ubiquiti gear end to end, and proper NAS storage. It starts with what's above.
