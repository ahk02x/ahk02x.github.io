The SQ5 build log

The overview says what is on the car. This is the order it went on, with the numbers each change moved where I have them. Part dates come from my order receipts and card statements, so a part's date is when I ordered it unless I say it went on.

January 2026

  • Air in. 034 S34 carbon intake with the aluminium turbo inlet, ordered January 13.
  • Chassis. 034 billet mount inserts (rear differential, rear subframe, transmission) and the Street Density transmission mount, January 26; 034 Dynamic+ lowering springs, January 28.

February 2026

  • Stance. CTS flush spacers, 15 mm front and 20 mm rear, picked up February 20, and the car aligned the same day.

June 2026

  • Tune ordered, June 10: Stage 2 for the engine and gearbox computers, with the flash cable that later became the logger's way into the car.
  • Air cooled. IE FDS intercooler, ordered June 12.
  • A gearbox-computer unlock, ordered June 15 and done July 13. The logger saw nothing: the request frames came back byte-identical, and shift firmness plays out over 100 to 300 ms, too quick for five reads a second to resolve.

July 2026

  • The logger. Reverse-engineered the diagnostic cable and wrote my own reader: one request every 0.18 s, about five a second. The first drive was logged July 4.
  • Stage 2 engine and gearbox tune. These drives are the baseline. Measured charge pressure peaks at about 23 to 24 psi between 4,500 and 5,500 rpm and tapers to about 21 by 6,000; wastegate duty sits at 87 to 95% at full throttle up top. That curve has not moved through October. The extra torque plus brake-boosting (building boost against the brake before a launch) put a lock-up clutch code, P1812, in the gearbox. I stopped brake-boosting.

August 2026

  • Pi onto the diagnostic port's always-on pin; power bank retired. The bank had cut the Pi dead for 4 h 41 min while charging and discharging at once. The pin is hot with the car off.
  • CAN tap for gearbox temperature. The engine-computer column I had labelled transmission temperature was intake air. The real number: 122 sessions, 16 to 102 °C, median trip maximum 74 °C.
  • First boot storm. 60 reboots in one parked day. On the alternator the Pi held ten minutes clean; on the battery every boot died at about 4.5 minutes. The 3.4 W draw I had planned around was an estimate, and it was off by about ten times.

September 2026

  • NGK Laser Iridium plugs. The change that mattered most. Warm misfire rate across all six cylinders went from 12.11 to 7.49 per 10,000 firings, 38% down, as a step at the plug boundary: seven weeks of 9.7 to 15.5 before, then 8.52, 8.43, 7.13. Cylinder 3 improved most, 3.89 to 2.01, but its ratio to its siblings went 2.37 to 1.83, unchanged within the noise.
  • Cylinder 3 plan. Ten weekly ratios of cylinder 3 to the pack: 1.52 to 3.63, mean about 2.7, flat. The plan: fit one new coil, keep the old one on the shelf, clear the codes, and judge the counter from zero. A ratio at or under 1.2 means fixed. Back in 1.9 to 3.6 means it was never the coil.
  • Away test passed. Five days off the home network: 27 drives held, shipped in about two minutes on rejoin. 243 drives, 592,917 samples.
  • Boot storms, the bad month. Peak 128 reboots in a day. They start 5 to 14.5 hours into a park, each boot lives 285 to 295 seconds, and an engine start ends it. The Pi was on 64 to 83% of parked hours.

October 2026

  • Cylinder 3 faded on its own. From about 2.3 times its siblings to 1.5 with no part changed. Even a perfect fix on cylinder 3 would remove at most 190 of the 1,407 misfire counts since the new plugs. The excess only shows at light load, 3.6 times below 25% load and nothing at 80% and up, which points at an injector, intake-valve deposits or a runner leak more than a coil.
  • Found a dead channel. The fuel-trim column has decoded to a constant since July. "Trim is +2.9%, so no vacuum leak" rested on nothing. Smoke test back on the list.
  • Knock got more frequent, not deeper. Warm cruise rows with any knock retard (timing pulled to stop knock): 0 to 2.5% on September days, 13.4% on the worst October day. A fuel batch is the lead; there is no fuel-level channel to check it.
  • Corrected my boost channel. I had been reporting the setpoint; measured pressure runs 145 to 173 hPa under it up top.

Not yet

  • The cylinder-3 coil. Not fitted. The counters have never reset.
  • Free reads first. Per-cylinder counts by physical cylinder, both banks' trims, a smoke test. A leakdown on cylinder 3 is the honest close.
  • The newer tune file. Boost 19.6 to 23.0 psi then flat from 5,500 rpm: spool, not power. After the coil verdict, because the flash resets the counters and the baseline takes 6 to 9 engine-hours to rebuild.
  • A hardware low-voltage cutoff for the Pi, with a current sensor so the draw is measured, not modelled. Until then: unplug on overnight parks, load-test the battery.
  • Parts. IE cat-back, IE charge pipe, 034 front strut brace, 034 sway bar bundle (one listing says air suspension only; confirming first), 034 two-piece rotors, 350×34 front and 330×22 rear, and a carbon hood.
